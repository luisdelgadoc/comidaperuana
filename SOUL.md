# ComidaPeruana — Project Soul

## What is ComidaPeruana?

ComidaPeruana is a visual gastronomic travel guide designed to help foreign travelers discover Peru through its food.

The product is not meant to be another restaurant directory, review platform, booking service, or alternative to Google Maps.

Its purpose is much simpler:

> Help someone visiting Peru understand what they should eat, why it matters, and where they can have a great version of it.

The core journey is:

**Destination → Dish → Recommended place**

Example:

**Lima → Ceviche → La Mar**

The dish is the main unit of discovery.

Restaurants exist to help the traveler experience that dish.

---

# Purpose

Peru has one of the world's most celebrated gastronomic cultures, but a traveler arriving for the first time often has very little context.

They may know that Peruvian food is good, but they may not know:

* what dishes they should try;
* which dishes belong to each city or region;
* why those dishes matter;
* what makes them culturally interesting;
* where they should try them;
* which recommendations are actually worth their limited travel time.

The internet already contains enormous amounts of information.

The problem is not a lack of information.

The opportunity is to provide:

**clarity, context, curation and confidence.**

ComidaPeruana should reduce hundreds of possible restaurants and dishes into a small number of thoughtful recommendations.

---

# Who are we building for?

Our initial user is:

> A foreign traveler who is planning or currently taking a trip to Peru and considers food an important part of the experience.

They are curious.

They may have heard that Peruvian cuisine is exceptional.

They are not necessarily food experts.

They do not want to spend hours researching restaurants.

They want someone knowledgeable to help them understand:

> "What should I eat while I'm here?"

and then:

> "Where should I try it?"

---

# The promise

ComidaPeruana should feel like receiving recommendations from a knowledgeable Peruvian friend.

Not:

> "Here are 342 restaurants near you."

Instead:

> "You're in Lima. These are the dishes worth knowing. If you want ceviche, these are three places we'd seriously consider."

We value **curation over abundance**.

We would rather recommend three excellent places than display fifty acceptable ones.

---

# Product philosophy

## 1. Start with the food

The platform should not begin with restaurants.

It should begin with Peruvian gastronomy.

The primary mental model is:

**What should I eat?**

Then:

**Where should I eat it?**

---

## 2. Fewer choices, better choices

More information does not automatically create a better experience.

Avoid overwhelming users with:

* large restaurant lists;
* dozens of filters;
* hundreds of reviews;
* endless categories;
* excessive metadata.

When possible, reduce complexity.

The product should help make decisions, not create more decisions.

---

## 3. Visual discovery

Food is emotional and visual.

Photography is not decoration.

It is part of the product.

Large images, strong typography and generous spacing should create curiosity before the user reads anything.

The interface should feel closer to:

* an editorial travel magazine;
* a premium food guide;
* a Typeform-style interactive experience;

than to:

* a directory;
* a marketplace;
* a SaaS dashboard;
* a traditional tourism portal.

---

## 4. Every screen should have a clear purpose

Avoid screens filled with competing actions.

Whenever possible:

**one screen → one primary decision.**

Examples:

> Where are you going?

> Where are you staying?

> What should you eat?

> Where should you try it?

The interface should guide the traveler progressively rather than presenting everything at once.

---

## 5. Editorial judgment matters

Google can provide:

* ratings;
* review counts;
* opening hours;
* maps;
* directions.

Those are useful signals.

But Google should not determine what we recommend.

Our recommendation layer should be editorial.

We decide:

* which dishes matter;
* which restaurants represent those dishes well;
* why a traveler should consider a place;
* what they should order there.

External ratings complement our recommendation.

They do not replace it.

---

# What ComidaPeruana is not

At least in its initial stages, ComidaPeruana is not:

* a social network;
* a user review community;
* a food delivery application;
* a reservation platform;
* a recipe website;
* an influencer platform;
* a restaurant marketplace;
* a gamification product;
* an AI chatbot;
* a generic travel planner.

Do not add these concepts simply because they are technically possible.

Every new feature must strengthen the core experience:

> Discover what to eat in Peru and where to experience it.

---

# Experience principles

When making UX decisions, prioritize:

1. Simplicity
2. Visual impact
3. Clarity
4. Curation
5. Speed
6. Mobile usability
7. Cultural context

The experience should feel:

* warm;
* contemporary;
* curious;
* premium without being elitist;
* distinctly connected to Peru;
* effortless.

It should never feel:

* corporate;
* cluttered;
* generic;
* overly technological;
* tourist-trap oriented.

---

# Content principles

Every dish should answer four questions:

### What is it?

A short and understandable explanation.

### Why should I try it?

Why this dish matters to someone visiting Peru.

### What should I know?

A small piece of useful cultural or local context.

### Where should I try it?

A carefully selected number of places.

Content should be concise.

Avoid encyclopedic writing.

We want to create curiosity, not reproduce Wikipedia.

---

# Tone of voice

The tone should be:

* knowledgeable;
* friendly;
* concise;
* confident without pretending there is only one correct answer;
* culturally respectful;
* accessible to someone unfamiliar with Peru.

Avoid exaggerated tourism language such as:

> "The greatest dish you will ever experience."

Prefer:

> "One of the dishes most closely associated with Lima's coastal cuisine."

The product should feel trustworthy rather than promotional.

---

# Visual soul

The interface should celebrate the food.

Use:

* oversized editorial typography;
* high-quality food photography;
* cinematic full-screen imagery;
* subtle motion;
* warm neutral backgrounds;
* restrained use of accent colors;
* generous negative space.

Transitions should reinforce the feeling of exploration.

Motion should be smooth and intentional.

Never add animation simply because it looks impressive.

---

# Mobile first

A traveler will often use ComidaPeruana while:

* walking;
* sitting in a hotel;
* planning over breakfast;
* deciding where to eat;
* already exploring the city.

The mobile experience is therefore the primary product.

Desktop is important, but mobile should never feel like a compressed desktop site.

---

# The first success moment

The first meaningful product outcome is not:

* creating an account;
* liking a restaurant;
* leaving a review.

It is:

> A traveler discovers a dish, sees a recommendation they trust, and opens the restaurant in Google Maps.

That means the product helped turn curiosity into a real-world gastronomic experience.

---

# Initial scope

The first version should focus on:

**Lima**

and later:

**Cusco**

A small number of destinations with excellent curation is more valuable than weak coverage of all Peru.

Do not optimize for geographic breadth too early.

---

# Long-term vision

If ComidaPeruana works, it could eventually become a way to explore Peru itself through gastronomy.

A traveler could move from:

**dish → restaurant**

to:

**dish → neighborhood → city → region → tradition → history → journey**

But the product should earn that complexity gradually.

The first version only needs to solve one question extremely well:

> "I'm here. What should I eat, and where should I try it?"

---

# Decision rule

Whenever there is uncertainty about what to build, choose the option that:

* reduces cognitive load;
* makes the food more visually prominent;
* makes recommendations more trustworthy;
* helps the traveler make a real-world decision;
* requires fewer steps;
* avoids unnecessary features.

When two implementations provide similar value, choose the simpler one.

---

# North Star

The long-term idea behind ComidaPeruana is:

> **Discover Peru through its food.**

Every product, content, design and engineering decision should ultimately support that idea.
